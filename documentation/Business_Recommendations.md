# IBM HR Analytics – Business Recommendations

## 1. Overview

This document presents business recommendations based on the findings from the exploratory data analysis (EDA) of the IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition & Performance dataset.

The recommendations focus on addressing employee attrition risks, improving employee engagement, strengthening retention strategies, and supporting data-driven Human Resources (HR) decisions.

The proposed actions are intended to help HR management prioritize employee groups that demonstrate relatively high attrition rates.

## 2. Recommended Business Actions

### 2.1 Strengthen Retention Strategies for Younger Employees

**Finding:**

Employees under 25 have the highest attrition rate at 39.18%, followed by employees aged 25–34 at 20.22%.

**Recommendations:**

- Develop structured onboarding programs for new and younger employees.
- Introduce mentoring programs that connect junior employees with experienced colleagues.
- Provide clear career development paths and promotion opportunities.
- Conduct regular check-ins during the first two years of employment.
- Collect feedback to identify challenges experienced by early-career employees.

**Expected Business Impact:**

Improved employee adaptation, stronger engagement, and potentially lower early-career attrition.

### 2.2 Prioritize Retention in High-Risk Job Roles

**Finding:**

Sales Representatives have the highest job-role attrition rate at 39.76%, while Laboratory Technicians contribute the largest number of attrition cases, with 62 employees.

**Recommendations:**

- Conduct role-specific employee satisfaction surveys.
- Review workload distribution and performance expectations.
- Evaluate compensation and incentive structures, particularly for Sales Representatives.
- Investigate working conditions and career progression opportunities for Laboratory Technicians.
- Conduct exit interviews to identify recurring reasons for employee departures.

**Expected Business Impact:**

Better understanding of role-specific retention challenges and more targeted HR interventions.

### 2.3 Improve Retention in the Sales Department

**Finding:**

The Sales department has the highest departmental attrition rate at 20.63%, compared with 13.84% in Research & Development and 19.05% in Human Resources.

**Recommendations:**

- Review sales targets and performance expectations.
- Evaluate the effectiveness and fairness of sales incentive programs.
- Monitor employee workload and work-related stress.
- Provide additional coaching and career development opportunities.
- Investigate attrition patterns across Sales job levels.

**Expected Business Impact:**

Improved employee retention and greater workforce stability within the Sales department.

### 2.4 Review Overtime and Workload Management

**Finding:**

Employees working overtime have an attrition rate of 30.53%, compared with 10.44% among employees who do not work overtime.

**Recommendations:**

- Monitor overtime frequency and duration across departments.
- Review workload allocation to prevent excessive workloads.
- Evaluate staffing requirements in teams with frequent overtime.
- Encourage managers to monitor employee workload and recovery time.
- Consider flexible working arrangements where operationally feasible.

**Expected Business Impact:**

A more balanced workload, reduced employee fatigue, and potentially improved employee retention.

### 2.5 Improve Employee Engagement and Job Involvement

**Finding:**

Employees with low job involvement have an attrition rate of 33.73%, while employees with very high job involvement have a rate of 9.03%.

**Recommendations:**

- Introduce regular employee engagement surveys.
- Encourage employee participation in team discussions and decision-making.
- Establish recognition programs for employee contributions.
- Provide opportunities for employees to develop new skills.
- Train managers to identify and address declining employee engagement.

**Expected Business Impact:**

Stronger employee involvement, improved workplace engagement, and potentially lower attrition.

### 2.6 Improve Job Satisfaction and Workplace Environment

**Finding:**

Employees with low job satisfaction have a 22.84% attrition rate. Employees with low environment satisfaction have a 25.35% attrition rate.

**Recommendations:**

- Conduct regular job satisfaction and workplace environment assessments.
- Identify recurring concerns related to management, communication, and working conditions.
- Improve communication between employees and supervisors.
- Develop action plans based on employee feedback.
- Monitor satisfaction levels over time to evaluate improvements.

**Expected Business Impact:**

A more supportive working environment and improved employee satisfaction.

### 2.7 Promote Better Work-Life Balance

**Finding:**

Employees reporting bad work-life balance have the highest attrition rate at 31.25%.

**Recommendations:**

- Review working schedules and workload distribution.
- Encourage managers to monitor signs of excessive work pressure.
- Consider flexible work arrangements where appropriate.
- Promote reasonable boundaries between work and personal time.
- Evaluate the relationship between overtime and work-life balance.

**Expected Business Impact:**

Improved employee well-being and potentially lower attrition among employees experiencing work-life balance challenges.

### 2.8 Strengthen Employee Retention During the First Years of Employment

**Finding:**

Employees with 0–2 years of company tenure have an attrition rate of 29.82%, compared with 6.67% among employees with 11–20 years of tenure.

**Recommendations:**

- Establish a structured employee onboarding and integration process.
- Schedule performance and development discussions during the first year.
- Assign mentors to newly hired employees.
- Provide clear expectations regarding responsibilities and career progression.
- Monitor early employee turnover by department and job role.

**Expected Business Impact:**

Better employee integration, improved early-career experience, and stronger long-term retention.

### 2.9 Review Compensation and Employee Benefits

**Finding:**

Employees earning below 3,000 have the highest attrition rate at 28.61%. Employees with Stock Option Level 0 have an attrition rate of 24.41%.

**Recommendations:**

- Review salary competitiveness across job roles and job levels.
- Evaluate compensation fairness and internal pay differences.
- Assess the effectiveness and accessibility of employee benefits.
- Review incentive and stock option programs.
- Consider compensation adjustments based on role responsibilities, performance, and market benchmarks.

**Expected Business Impact:**

Improved compensation competitiveness and a better understanding of how rewards and benefits relate to employee retention.

### 2.10 Investigate Business Travel and Commute-Related Challenges

**Finding:**

Employees who travel frequently have an attrition rate of 24.91%. Employees living 21+ miles from work have an attrition rate of 22.06%.

**Recommendations:**

- Review the frequency and demands of business travel.
- Evaluate travel schedules and opportunities to reduce unnecessary trips.
- Consider flexible working arrangements where possible.
- Collect employee feedback about commuting difficulties.
- Investigate whether travel frequency and commute distance overlap with other attrition risk factors.

**Expected Business Impact:**

Better understanding of travel and commuting challenges, with opportunities to improve employee experience.

### 2.11 Develop Career Growth and Internal Mobility Opportunities

**Finding:**

Employees at Job Level 1 have the highest attrition rate at 26.34%. Employees who left also have shorter average tenure in their current roles than active employees.

**Recommendations:**

- Establish transparent career progression frameworks.
- Provide training and professional development programs.
- Encourage internal job applications and mobility.
- Conduct regular career discussions between managers and employees.
- Identify employees who may benefit from role changes or additional responsibilities.

**Expected Business Impact:**

Improved career development opportunities and stronger employee commitment to the organization.

## 4. Suggested HR Monitoring Metrics

To evaluate the effectiveness of the recommended actions, HR management should monitor the following metrics regularly.

| Metric | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Overall Attrition Rate | Monitor changes in employee turnover |
| Department Attrition Rate | Identify departments with increasing turnover |
| Job Role Attrition Rate | Monitor retention challenges across job roles |
| New Employee Attrition Rate | Evaluate early employee retention |
| Overtime Attrition Rate | Monitor attrition among employees working overtime |
| Average Employee Tenure | Track workforce stability |
| Employee Job Satisfaction | Evaluate employee satisfaction trends |
| Work-Life Balance Score | Monitor employee work-life balance |
| Internal Promotion Rate | Evaluate career development opportunities |
| Employee Engagement Score | Monitor employee involvement and engagement |

These metrics should be reviewed periodically and compared with previous reporting periods.

## 5. Implementation Roadmap

| Phase | Recommended Activities | Objective |
|---|---|---|
| Short-Term | Review overtime, conduct employee surveys, analyze exit interviews | Identify immediate retention challenges |
| Medium-Term | Improve onboarding, mentoring, career development, and workload management | Address recurring attrition patterns |
| Long-Term | Evaluate compensation strategy, internal mobility, and retention programs | Establish sustainable employee retention practices |

## 6. Conclusion

The analysis highlights several areas that deserve attention from HR management, including overtime, early employee tenure, compensation, job involvement, work-life balance, and retention within specific job roles.

By implementing targeted retention initiatives and monitoring relevant HR metrics, the organization can better understand employee turnover patterns and make more informed workforce management decisions.

The recommendations should be treated as proposed actions rather than guaranteed solutions. Their effectiveness should be evaluated through continuous monitoring, employee feedback, and comparison of attrition metrics over time.
